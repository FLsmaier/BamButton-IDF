# ESP32-C3 Super Mini WiFi

Close-range connection errors on the ESP32-C3 Super Mini are explained by the board: a tiny ceramic chip antenna on a 22.5 × 18 mm PCB, with the keep-out and matching Espressif requires for a 2.4 GHz antenna squeezed out, and on some units a 3.3 V rail that sags when the radio transmits. They are not explained by the usual ESP-IDF station setup. This project already turns modem sleep off, sets country `DE` with 802.11d disabled after `esp_wifi_start`, and does not force the radio back to channel 1 after the station associates. A softAP configured on channel 1 does not pin the station to channel 1. The station channel wins, and the softAP follows it.

Reports below that give RSSI numbers are blog, forum, or hobby-test write-ups. They are labeled as such. None of them is an Espressif chamber measurement of this board.

## What antenna the Super Mini has

The common board is a third-party bare-chip board, about 22.52 × 18 mm, built around an ESP32-C3FN4 with an onboard regulator the pinout notes call an ME6211. That description is from a board repo that checked a physical unit and cites the nologo SuperMini page ([Mi-Bee Studio README](https://github.com/Mi-Bee-Studio/esp32-c3-mini/blob/main/README.md)). The same note calls the radiator an “onboard PCB antenna.” Rework write-ups of the same ~22.5 × 18 mm boards describe a ceramic SMD chip, not a meander etched in the copper and not an IPEX connector on the usual variant.

Peter Neufeld’s antenna-mod write-up identifies that chip as a CrossAir CA-C03 and says it sits too close to the ground pour ([blog](https://peterneufeld.wordpress.com/2025/03/04/esp32-c3-supermini-antenna-modification/)). A community KiCad redraw of the AliExpress board says the original part is the same CA-C03, used with no matching network, and replaces it with a Johanson 2450AT18A100 plus a T-match ([ajz811/ESP32-C3-Super-Mini-Kicad](https://github.com/ajz811/ESP32-C3-Super-Mini-Kicad)). Those are reverse-engineering claims, not a manufacturer BOM. They agree with each other, and with the CA-C03 specification, that this is a ceramic chip.

The CA-C03 datasheet (English PDF) gives a 5.5 × 2.0 × 1.0 mm SMD patch, working frequency 2450 ± 50 MHz, an “initial frequency band” of 2.7 GHz, 50 Ω, VSWR < 2, and a peak gain of 4.3 dBi. The match it specifies is series 0 Ω, shunt 3 nH, series 0 Ω. Gain and efficiency (peak 81.7 %) are measured on the vendor’s test board, 1.0 mm thick ([CA-C03 specification](https://www.mikrocontroller.net/attachment/691153/CA-C03.pdf)). A Chinese CA-C03 sheet says the 2.4 GHz operating frequency has to be reached by tuning those matching parts ([CA-C03 Chinese specification](https://fccid.ai/m/be47aaa0b97efed9645126429207f2bb548251de1456ec8c6b9517bc4c645a19.pdf)). The 4.3 dBi figure is that test board, not a measurement of a Super Mini. Peter also says the datasheet requires a 3.8 mm × 0.5 mm stripline; that dimension is not in the text of the English PDF above (the pad drawing did not extract as text), so it stays his reading of the drawing.

Espressif’s own comparison puts a ceramic chip in the “low gain, small size” class and a PCB antenna in the “medium gain, usually integrated on modules” class ([ESP32-C3 book, RF and antenna](https://espressif.github.io/esp32-c3-book-en/chapter_5/5.2/5.2.5.html)).

The Espressif module this board is often confused with is different. ESP32-C3-MINI-1 is a 13.2 × 16.6 × 2.4 mm module with an on-board PCB antenna and a marked antenna keep-out. ESP32-C3-MINI-1U has a connector and no keep-out. Conducted Wi-Fi numbers in that datasheet are taken at the antenna port through a cable, so they are not the radiated gain of the PCB antenna. The 2.33 dBi cap in the same datasheet is the maximum gain of the external antenna used to certify the connector variant ([ESP32-C3-MINI-1 datasheet](https://documentation.espressif.com/esp32-c3-mini-1_datasheet_en.html)).

Espressif’s layout rules for that PCB antenna: put the antenna outside the base board, feed point at the edge, cut the base board away on both sides of the antenna and underneath it, keep a solid ground and dense ground vias beside the antenna, and leave at least 15 mm clear in every direction inside the housing ([PCB layout design](https://docs.espressif.com/projects/esp-hardware-design-guidelines/en/latest/esp32c3/pcb-layout-design.html)). The whole Super Mini is 22.52 × 18 mm ([Mi-Bee README](https://github.com/Mi-Bee-Studio/esp32-c3-mini/blob/main/README.md)), so a 15 mm clearance in all directions does not fit on the board. The same guide says the USB connector and UART traces must be as far from the antenna as possible, and that a PCB antenna needs its own CLC match unless simulation already shows 50 Ω.

Some shop variants do add an IPEX socket. Peter’s comparison of several Super Mini antenna sections says those boards need a solder jumper to enable the socket, and the SMD chip still has to come off ([blog](https://peterneufeld.wordpress.com/2025/03/04/esp32-c3-supermini-antenna-modification/)). A GitHub discussion is the same report from one owner: a 0 Ω link selects the chip or the IPEX, and both paths must not be connected at once ([arduino-esp32 discussion #12767](https://github.com/espressif/arduino-esp32/discussions/12767)). That discussion is a user report.

## Weak RSSI and dropouts at close range

Yes. The reports are consistent that the stock board is weak next to an access point, and that a wire or a different antenna moves the RSSI. They do not agree on a single number, and none of them is a calibrated lab.

Peter’s blog (his own RSSI logger, two Super Minis side by side on a power bank, not a chamber): the wire mod was “at least approximately 6 dB” better on average, “often 6 dB to 8 dB” close to the AP, and “often reached 10 dB or more” farther away. The unmodified board dropped the link in places where the modified one held it. Moving the stock SMD chip to a freer spot, still on the board, was “an approximate 4 dB improvement.” A later forum follow-up on the same mod shows a “constant 5 dB difference” for the repositioned chip, and “+6 dB to +10 dB” for the wire ([blog](https://peterneufeld.wordpress.com/2025/03/04/esp32-c3-supermini-antenna-modification/), [Annex RDS forum](https://cicciocb.com/forum/viewtopic.php?start=10&t=1477)).

Home Assistant forum, anecdote, davidmonro, January 2024, AP management RSSI, desk directly under a ceiling AP: stock Super Mini “around -62 dBm”, same board after a wire loop “around -45 dBm”, and a different ESP32-C3 with a PCB trace antenna in the same place running the same binary “-33 dBm”. Two of his Super Minis would not connect on channel 11 and were only weakly usable on channel 1 until the wire; channel 11 then worked. He reads that as a bad match, with TX energy reflected into the board ([thread](https://community.home-assistant.io/t/esp32-c3-super-mini-wifi-signal-booster/657661)). Another post in that thread only says “20% or less” in another room, with no dBm.

A three-board hobby write-up (every value an average of three readings, devices about 49 inches apart) measured one stock ceramic Super Mini at −47.00 dBm from the home router while an ESP32-S3 DevKitC beside the test read −29.00 dBm from that same router. As a transmitter, that stock board’s softAP was seen at −69.00 dBm by an ESP32-WROOM-32E and −63.67 dBm by the S3, while the home router at those same receivers was about −29 to −30 dBm ([espnow-mesh-system test notes](https://github.com/atechofficials/espnow-mesh-system/blob/main/docs/esp32_c3_supermini_wifi_tests/README.md)). That is one person’s three boards, two of them already reworked, so it shows a large gap and a lot of board-to-board scatter. It is not a survey.

## Hardware causes

Espressif’s schematic rules for the ESP32-C3 RF path are a 50 Ω trace, a CLC match at the chip, and a CLC match at the antenna unless the antenna is already simulated at 50 Ω. Running the radio with no antenna connected “may result in unstable behavior or potential damage to the RF circuit” ([schematic checklist](https://docs.espressif.com/projects/esp-hardware-design-guidelines/en/latest/esp32c3/schematic-checklist.html)). The layout guide’s failure write-up for “power much higher or lower than the target, EVM poor” is impedance mismatch on the trace to the antenna: reflected power drives the PA into saturation early ([PCB layout design](https://docs.espressif.com/projects/esp-hardware-design-guidelines/en/latest/esp32c3/pcb-layout-design.html)). The CA-C03 sheet requires the 3 nH shunt on its test board ([CA-C03 specification](https://www.mikrocontroller.net/attachment/691153/CA-C03.pdf)). Peter and the KiCad redraw both say the Super Mini omits that match and parks the chip against the ground ([blog](https://peterneufeld.wordpress.com/2025/03/04/esp32-c3-supermini-antenna-modification/), [KiCad redraw](https://github.com/ajz811/ESP32-C3-Super-Mini-Kicad)).

Ground and keep-out are the same guide: four layers preferred, two layers only with a continuous ground under the chip, the RF trace, and the crystal; at least nine ground vias in the chip ground pad; antenna at the board edge with copper cut away beside and under it; 15 mm clearance ([PCB layout design](https://docs.espressif.com/projects/esp-hardware-design-guidelines/en/latest/esp32c3/pcb-layout-design.html)). A 22.52 × 18 mm single-sided board ([Mi-Bee README](https://github.com/Mi-Bee-Studio/esp32-c3-mini/blob/main/README.md)) cannot meet the 15 mm rule. How much ground copper a given Super Mini actually has is not in an Espressif document; the public evidence is the placement criticism above.

TX current is large enough to collapse a weak 3.3 V rail. At 3.3 V and 25 °C the ESP32-C3 datasheet lists a peak of 335 mA for 802.11b 1 Mbps at 21 dBm, 285 mA for 802.11g 54 Mbps at 19 dBm, and 276 mA for 802.11n HT20 MCS7 at 18.5 dBm. RX is about 84 mA ([ESP32-C3 datasheet](https://documentation.espressif.com/esp32-c3_datasheet_en.html)). The hardware guidelines say a single 3.3 V supply should provide at least 500 mA, and that TX current steps on VDD3P3 can collapse the rail unless there is a 10 µF bulk capacitor beside the 0.1 µF decouplers ([hardware design guidelines, power supply](https://documentation.espressif.com/esp-hardware-design-guidelines/en/latest/esp32c3/esp-hardware-design-guidelines-en-master-esp32c3.pdf)). Espressif’s book adds ripple limits: under 80 mV peak for MCS7, under 120 mV for 11 Mbit/s ([power-supply guidelines](https://espressif.github.io/esp32-c3-book-en/chapter_5/5.3/5.3.5.html)). The three-board test above is the Super Mini-specific evidence: one board’s softAP was invisible until it was powered from an external 3.3 V supply, and two boards transmitted more reliably after the onboard LDO was replaced with a MIC5219. That author treats the stock LDO as the weak part on those two boards ([test notes](https://github.com/atechofficials/espnow-mesh-system/blob/main/docs/esp32_c3_supermini_wifi_tests/README.md)). There is no Espressif measurement of the Super Mini regulator.

The ESP32-C3 chip errata (v1.3 summary) lists a misaligned-access fault and two SAR ADC DMA bugs. There is no Wi-Fi or RF erratum ([errata summary](https://docs.espressif.com/projects/esp-chip-errata/en/latest/esp32c3/02-errata-summary/index.html)). Variant scatter is real and it is not an Espressif module lot: Peter shows several antenna layouts in the shops, including IPEX versions, and says they still have short range ([blog](https://peterneufeld.wordpress.com/2025/03/04/esp32-c3-supermini-antenna-modification/)). The forum report separates “black/white” chip-antenna boards from “red antenna” boards that measured stronger with the same binary ([Home Assistant thread](https://community.home-assistant.io/t/esp32-c3-super-mini-wifi-signal-booster/657661)).

## Firmware causes that flap a healthy radio

These are real, and they can fail association with a strong AP. They are properties of the ESP-IDF Wi-Fi driver, on every ESP32-C3, including a MINI-1.

**One channel in APSTA.** “ESP devices are limited to only one channel, so when in the soft-AP+station mode, the soft-AP will adjust its channel automatically to be the same as the channel of the station” ([ESP32-C3 `esp_wifi_set_config`](https://docs.espressif.com/projects/esp-idf/en/stable/esp32c3/api-reference/network/esp_wifi.html)). The mode overview says the external AP’s channel has priority over the ESP softAP channel. If the softAP was on channel 6 and the station joins an AP on channel 9, the softAP moves to 9 and announces it with a channel-switch announcement ([Wi-Fi driver overview, Home Channel](https://github.com/espressif/esp-idf/blob/v6.0.2/docs/en/api-guides/wifi-driver/overview.rst)). `esp_wifi_set_channel` must not be called while the station is scanning or connecting, or while the softAP has clients ([same API page](https://docs.espressif.com/projects/esp-idf/en/stable/esp32c3/api-reference/network/esp_wifi.html)).

This project sets the softAP channel field to 1 and never calls `esp_wifi_set_channel` (`main/bb_wifi.c`). That configures the softAP until the station associates. It does not hold the station on channel 1. After the station joins, both roles sit on the upstream AP’s channel. The clients of the setup AP are what move or drop.

**Country and channels 12–13.** Until something sets a country, the default is world-safe `"01"`: `{.cc="01", .schan=1, .nchan=11, .policy=WIFI_COUNTRY_POLICY_AUTO}`, and 802.11d follows the AP ([`esp_wifi_set_country`](https://docs.espressif.com/projects/esp-idf/en/stable/esp32c3/api-reference/network/esp_wifi.html)). `"DE"` is in the supported-code list. With `ieee80211d_enabled` false, “the configured country info is used always” ([`esp_wifi_set_country_code`](https://docs.espressif.com/projects/esp-idf/en/stable/esp32c3/api-reference/network/esp_wifi.html)). ESP-IDF’s regulatory entry for DE is 2400–2483.5 MHz at 100 mW, the 2.4 GHz band that contains channels 1–13 ([`esp_wifi_regulatory.txt`](https://github.com/espressif/esp-idf/blob/v6.0.2/components/esp_wifi/regulatory/esp_wifi_regulatory.txt)). Espressif’s FAQ: from IDF 5.0 the default is `"01"` with 11 channels; channels 12 and 13 are passive-scanned by default, so a hidden SSID on 12 or 13 is missed unless the policy is manual ([Wi-Fi FAQ](https://docs.espressif.com/projects/esp-faq/en/latest/software-framework/wifi.html)). In 2022 Espressif changed `esp_wifi_set_country_code` so it only works after `esp_wifi_start` ([esp-idf #9521](https://github.com/espressif/esp-idf/issues/9521)). This project calls `esp_wifi_set_country_code("DE", false)` after `esp_wifi_start` (`main/bb_wifi.c`), which is that order, and which disables 802.11d.

**Modem sleep and listen interval.** The default power-save mode is `WIFI_PS_MIN_MODEM`: the station wakes for every DTIM beacon. `WIFI_PS_MAX_MODEM` wakes on `listen_interval` (AP beacon intervals, default 3 if left 0). `WIFI_PS_NONE` is no power save ([`esp_wifi_set_ps`](https://docs.espressif.com/projects/esp-idf/en/stable/esp32c3/api-reference/network/esp_wifi.html), [low-power Wi-Fi guide](https://docs.espressif.com/projects/esp-idf/en/stable/esp32/api-guides/low-power-mode/low-power-mode-wifi.html)). `listen_interval` applies only in `WIFI_PS_MAX_MODEM`. This project sets `WIFI_PS_NONE` after start (`main/bb_wifi.c`), so DTIM sleep and the listen interval are not in the path.

**TX power.** `esp_wifi_set_max_tx_power` is only valid after Wi-Fi start. The argument is in 0.25 dBm steps from 8 to 84, meaning 2 dBm to 20 dBm, and it is stored as the country `max_tx_power`. Before start, the cap is the PHY init data ([API reference](https://docs.espressif.com/projects/esp-idf/en/stable/esp32c3/api-reference/network/esp_wifi.html)). This project calls it after start and after the country code: 34 (8.5 dBm) unless the stored setting turns the cap off, in which case it sets 84 (20 dBm). A separate advisory, AR2024-002, describes a 20 dB TX-power drop, only when three things are true together: chip revision v0.1 through v1.0, an affected PHY (about v1000–v1130 / specific IDF builds), and either modified PHY init data or `CONFIG_ESP_PHY_MULTIPLE_INIT_DATA_BIN`. Revision v1.1 is unaffected ([AR2024-002](https://documentation.espressif.com/ar2024-002_en.html)). That is a firmware/PHY footgun on those chips. It is not the Super Mini antenna, and it is not the default PHY init data.

**Auth threshold.** `wifi_scan_threshold_t.rssi` “defaults to -127 if set to >= 0”, so a zeroed RSSI field does not reject a strong AP. The auth-mode field rejects networks weaker than the mode you set ([API reference](https://docs.espressif.com/projects/esp-idf/en/stable/esp32c3/api-reference/network/esp_wifi.html)). This project sets WPA2 when a password is configured, and PMF capable but not required (`main/bb_wifi.c`). That refuses open, WEP, and WPA1 networks. It does not flap a WPA2 AP that is already in range.

The hobby test that capped Arduino `WIFI_POWER_8_5dBm` after startup, and only then saw some reworked Super Minis transmit, is a workaround for those boards’ PA, match, or regulator. The same note says the cap did nothing when it was applied too early ([test notes](https://github.com/atechofficials/espnow-mesh-system/blob/main/docs/esp32_c3_supermini_wifi_tests/README.md)). That matches the API rule that the power set before start is not the runtime cap.

## How to tell the board from the config

Read RSSI on the upstream AP, and compare with a module that has a real antenna, running the same binary, in the same spot.

**Board / antenna / regulator.** RSSI stays poor with the AP in the same room. The forum anecdote above is about −62 dBm directly under the AP; the three-board test is about 18 dB worse on receive than an S3 next to it, and about 35 dB worse on transmit than the home router at the same receiver. The same binary on a PCB-trace ESP32-C3 was about −33 dBm where the Super Mini was about −62 dBm. A wire, a moved chip, or another antenna improves both directions by several dB, including at close range (Peter’s 6–8 dB near the AP). Failure can be worse on some channels and still be the antenna: the forum boards that refused channel 11 and were weak on channel 1 started working on channel 11 after the wire, with no firmware change. TX worse than RX (the AP hears the board poorly, or the board’s softAP is invisible, while scan still sees beacons) matches reflected power and a PA or rail that folds on transmit. Swapping in a stiffer 3.3 V source, or the link working from a bench supply and failing from USB, points at the regulator. Another Super Mini from a different seller behaving differently points at the variant, which Peter and the forum both describe.

**APSTA, channel, country, or power save.** RSSI next to the AP looks like a normal client (the forum commenter’s expectation for “on top of the router” was about −33 dBm, and that is what their PCB-trace board actually reported). The station joins when the upstream AP is on the softAP’s channel and the setup-AP clients die, or roam, when the station then associates to a different channel. That is the single-radio rule, not the ceramic chip. The station never sees an AP that is known to be on channel 12 or 13, and the country still reads `01` or the scan of those channels is passive. That is the world-safe default. The link is fine in `WIFI_PS_NONE` and drops beacons or stalls under `WIFI_PS_MIN_MODEM` / `WIFI_PS_MAX_MODEM`. A MINI-1 or other module with the same firmware shows the same channel or sleep failure, which removes the Super Mini antenna from the explanation. A 20 dB TX-only hole on chip revision v0.1–v1.0 with a modified PHY init blob is AR2024-002, and the boot log’s PHY version is the check.

For this tree, the second pattern’s usual switches are already in the off position: `WIFI_PS_NONE`, `esp_wifi_set_country_code("DE", false)` after start, no `esp_wifi_set_channel`, no RSSI floor. The softAP channel of 1 still matters for whoever is joined to the setup AP. It does not explain a station that cannot stay associated to a strong AP on some other channel.
